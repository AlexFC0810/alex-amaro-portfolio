Stage 04 · Recover · the second input

Your CRM is a market you already paid for.

Every lead that did not book, every call that rang out, every no-show, every enquiry from two winters ago — that is demand with the acquisition cost already sunk. Most accounts never work it. The systems below are built to.

Reactivation, two years on

730+ tattoo-removal leads at ~$19–$21 CPL in a saturated NYC market — and two years on, the follow-up system I built there was still working: in March 2026 it re-worked 468 aged contacts through a fresh reactivation offer. The system outlived the campaign that filled it.

Saved smart lists for warm, aged and dated cohorts — the difference between a worked database and a raw dump.

Missed-call text-back

A call that rings out becomes a text within the minute, a task for a human and a pipeline record — one compound step rather than three workflows that can each fail separately. Built into every client engine as a standard component.

No-show recovery, and the lesson

Cancellation and no-show fall-outs pulled back into sequence instead of leaking. The lesson came from the aesthetic-clinic account, where booking was not the bottleneck and attendance was: the fix field-tested afterwards was a small deposit at the point of booking — you book slightly fewer and keep materially more.

Deposit-taking, live in a client CRM

Aged leads, dialled

On a med spa account the lead-aging process carried the largest enrolment count in the CRM: per-offer SMS follow-up, per-offer team alerting, a dialler working aged leads, opt-out compliance shipped as a build component. Lead aging is the unglamorous half of speed-to-lead — it works the contact who did not answer on day one.

That account’s platform record is gone; its cost figure is held, not quoted. The build is what survived.

The operator · few handoffs

Where the leverage actually came from.

A growth problem normally changes hands four or five times between the signal and the measurement. Here it mostly does not. I ran my own agency with a four-person delivery team — call center, media buyer, ad creative and GoHighLevel — before rebuilding that same capability as AI systems. The software is built and maintained inside agentic coding environments; what to build, and whether the number it produces can be defended, is not delegated.

Agentic research on a cron

An opportunity radar in TypeScript on a 30-minute GitHub Actions cron: reads a saved-search inbox over IMAP, parses, scores against a rubric, drafts with a prompt-cached template, posts to Slack with the proposal attached, and marks the message so dedup survives a stateless run. No public URL — it holds live credentials.

The judgment call: version one read the official API, whose key sits behind an approval gate that can stall for months and once did. A perfect input that never turns on is worth zero, so the input became the inbox — and that single change deleted the database, the bot and the always-on server.

A generative creative system, gated

The creative above came out of a system rather than a session: a brief with five falsifiable fields written before generation — hypothesis, control, metric, who reports it, stop condition — a library of 32 named direction modes mined from 516 prior prompts, and a review build that fails on any variant that cannot name what it varies against the control. The ledger is kept honest by keeping the losers: 109 generations, 12 accepted, 25 rejected, 72 not yet judged, and only two market verdicts — which is the gap between generating and proving, stated rather than hidden.

The system stays in-house. What it produces is on this page and on the wall; “no winner” is a legitimate outcome, and three in a row fires a self-kill trigger.

An operator console, and a worked audit

A back-office console (Next.js, Supabase, three daily crons: scrape, enrich, generate outreach, push to CRM) whose shell is public and whose data is behind a sign-in — you will see the door. Judgment, on the record

Six things I refused, corrected or withdrew.

Anyone can generate impressive artifacts. The senior signal is what you decline to claim. These are on the case pages and the audit, in more detail than here.

Retired the portfolio-wide appointment total.

The census was real — appointment rows present in systems I built — but measured is not attributable: some of those systems carried the clients’ own front-desk bookings. I declined to defend the inference, at any size. Bookings are claimed per client, tied to the campaign that produced them.

Made the currency travel with the number.

A Canadian account’s cost per lead was once published unmarked; a U.S. reader takes CA$ for US$ and I looked roughly 27% more expensive than I was. Corrected in the contract, everywhere, at once.

Quarantined the awareness spend.

On the B2B account the launch campaign bought reach cheaply and is excluded from every cost-per-lead figure. The $1.88 gets no help from it — and even dividing every pound in the account by the lead count stays under the published claim.

Kept the finding that made me look wrong.

Two lead-capture routes run against each other on one service line: the native instant form turned out to be the more expensive one. The result stayed in the record rather than being quietly dropped.

Never divided a programme by its leads.

A $145K three-year programme ran brand, traffic and lead generation as three funded layers. Most of the spend bought reach and clicks by design; a cost per lead derived from it would be arithmetic on two unrelated numbers. And the second location that was set up and never ran is recorded, not hidden.

Built the tripwire after a picture leaked what no text search could find.

A screenshot of an internal review page shipped three legible generation prompts to a public URL. The review site was gated; the picture of it was not. The fix was mechanical: a hashed leak tripwire and a build that refuses any figure the contract does not hold, on every push.

What this page does not claim

  • Not client revenue, and not ROAS. I do not have post-booking visibility, and closing is the client’s side of the line. What I claim is leads, booked appointments and the follow-up systems between them. The two economics columns above are illustrative arithmetic, labelled as such.
  • Not a blended cost per lead. Total spend divided by total leads is not a CPL and I will not quote one.
  • Not scaled AI voice. Shipped, and ran, from February 2025 — at single-digit to low-double enrolment volumes. The demo line is a demo line.
  • Not client identities. Clients appear by vertical and country only.
  • Not a portfolio-wide appointment total. Bookings are claimed per client, tied to the campaigns that produced them.
