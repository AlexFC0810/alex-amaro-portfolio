Aesthetics, FR/EN · Canada

Bilingual acquisition in Montréal, held in-language all the way to the calendar

The conversion risk in this account sat after the click, in the follow-up layer — which is precisely the layer most media buyers hand back to the client.

The problem

What this business was actually up against.

Two service lines with opposite buyer psychology inside one clinic. One is price-led, high-consideration and sold as a sequence of visits; the other is trust-led, lower volume, higher ticket and gated by a clinical consult. Averaging them into one campaign and one cost target would have produced a number that described neither.

Underneath that sat the harder problem: language. A lead can opt in from a French ad and go cold because the confirmation text arrives in English, or the reverse.

The clinic also had a practice-management platform it was not going to abandon. Whatever got built had to generate and work the leads without trying to replace the system of record.

What I built

The mechanism, step by step.

Not a list of tools. The decisions that produced the outcome, in the order they were made.

1

Split the account by service line, not by budget

Two distinct lead-gen campaigns with separate creative, separate economics and separate cost signals — because a laser lead and an injectables lead are not the same unit.

2

Built the offer, not just the media buy

A structural qualifying offer on the higher-volume line that selects for people ready to start a treatment sequence and hands the clinic a natural first-visit conversation. A second offer track ran on the other service line, with its own named follow-up sequence.

3

Ran prospecting and retargeting as separate jobs

A dated prospecting set, a static set, and a dedicated review-carousel retargeting set — social proof deployed where it converts rather than burned on cold traffic.

4

Ran acquisition and follow-up in-language

This is visible in the data rather than asserted: the lead records carry French-Canadian names and .fr addresses, and the tracker’s own operational status notes are written in French. The bilingual layer was operational, not cosmetic.

5

Worked with the client’s system of record rather than against it

One workflow is literally titled to record that appointment reminders were removed because the clinic’s own platform already sent them. That integration decision is why the CRM calendar under-counts — a fact worth volunteering, because it explains a discrepancy an auditor would otherwise find first.

6

Instrumented every lead

Each tracker row carries the campaign that produced it, the confirmation method, and a show / no-show / rescheduled / cancelled status. Ad to lead to follow-up to booked appointment, logged per lead.

The boundary

What this case does not claim.

This section exists because it is the part that survives an interview. Stating the limits before anyone has to ask is what makes the rest of the page worth believing.

The cost per lead is permanently retired

The ad account was revoked at off-boarding and the exact figure is unrecoverable. It is not estimated, not reconstructed, and not quoted — on any surface, in any form.

“Booked” means scheduled

Not attended, not held. Outcome splits are quoted only where they were actually logged.

No client revenue and no return on ad spend

The franchise’s treatment revenue is its own. Closing is client-side.

The lead figure is founder-attested at its floor

It is corroborated by a photographed CRM total that is higher. The lower number is the one published.
