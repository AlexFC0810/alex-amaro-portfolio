A multi-location group running three marketing jobs at once

Be known in two catchment areas, be visited, and be booked. Those are three jobs, and a multi-objective account is exactly where blended maths dies.

The problem

What this business was actually up against.

Most of this programme’s spend never optimised for a form fill and was never asked to. Dividing it by a lead count would produce a cost per lead that describes nothing that happened.

So the programme was structured by objective from the start, with each layer funded and measured on its own terms.

That structure is also what makes the account auditable years later — the layers never got averaged into one number that nobody could take apart.

What I built

The mechanism, step by step.

Not a list of tools. The decisions that produced the outcome, in the order they were made.

1

Structured the programme by objective into three funded layers

Brand and awareness, traffic and consideration, and lead generation — kept as three sets of campaigns rather than averaged into one account-level number.

2

Built campaigns per catchment location

A second CRM sub-account stood up for the second location within three days of the first, so each catchment carried its own funnel rather than sharing one.

3

Named and priced the offer, and built the matching follow-up sequence

The creative price and the workflow name agree across two systems — which is what makes an offer claim checkable rather than remembered.

4

Deployed deposit-taking at the point of booking

The show-rate fix, present as a live artifact in the client’s CRM rather than as an interview anecdote.

5

Shipped lead-quality hygiene as a standard build component

Spam filtering inside the funnel, so the front desk is not the filter.

6

Wired pipeline stage changes back to the ad platform

Stage-triggered conversion events sent back to the channel, closing the measurement loop.

The boundary

What this case does not claim.

This section exists because it is the part that survives an interview. Stating the limits before anyone has to ask is what makes the rest of the page worth believing.

Only the tagged subset is counted

655 leads at $20.95 is a floor drawn from the campaigns carrying Alex's own tag inside a $144,918.33 account. Wider readings exist — 735 leads at $24.92, 887 at $23.70 — but every campaign they add performed worse than the ones already counted, so the safest reading here is also the best-performing one. Attribution is campaign-level; ad-set and ad-level are not yet applied.

Not all of this account is his work

The account predates the engagement and contains untagged campaigns. Only the tag-attributable subset is his; the rest is described as spend managed and supported, never as work produced.

Programme spend is never divided by a lead total

Most of the spend bought reach and clicks, not form fills. Any cost per lead derived that way would be arithmetic on two unrelated numbers.

The second location never ran

It was set up and produced nothing. Recorded deliberately, because an honest census is what makes the rest of the portfolio credible.
