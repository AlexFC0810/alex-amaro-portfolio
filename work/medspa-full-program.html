A full med spa programme across four service lines

A med spa selling medical weight loss, Botox, laser and photofacial is not one advertiser. It is four — and the calendar can fill with people who will never buy the high-ticket protocol.

The problem

What this business was actually up against.

The clinic sold four very different things to four very different buyers. Volume in a category where the cheap lead and the qualified lead are not the same lead.

The failure mode is picking the easiest service line, running it, and presenting that as a programme — which leaves three quarters of the business unserved and the account’s real economics unknown.

It needed to be built as a programme: every line funded, every line measured separately, and the winners scaled without letting cost drift.

What I built

The mechanism, step by step.

Not a list of tools. The decisions that produced the outcome, in the order they were made.

1

Built as a programme, not a campaign

Twenty campaigns across five service lines, the majority of them weight-loss builds — each line carrying its own cost signal rather than being averaged into one account number.

2

A legible, auditable naming convention

Date and budget stamps plus an agency prefix, separating his work from anyone else’s in the same account. This is what makes an account re-auditable two years later.

3

Iterated on a visible dated cadence, and scaled winners without drift

The biggest campaigns were also among the cheapest — which is the inverse of the usual pattern and the thing that is hard to do.

4

Killed losers small

Four weight-loss tests stayed small and stayed expensive, and were stopped. Winners and losers are both left in the record.

5

Tested lead-capture mechanics and kept the counter-intuitive finding

Two routes were run against each other on the same service line, and the native instant form turned out to be the more expensive one. The finding was kept rather than quietly dropped.

6

Produced the creative and designed the offers

Including a priced voucher used as a qualifying mechanism rather than a discount, and qualification at the form on which service and how soon.

7

Built the CRM follow-up layer as one system

An evergreen new-lead workflow, a named offer sequence, appointment reminders, reply-intent routing and a custom reporting dashboard.

The boundary

What this case does not claim.

This section exists because it is the part that survives an interview. Stating the limits before anyone has to ask is what makes the rest of the page worth believing.

No revenue, no return on ad spend, no package sales

Closing is client-side. No patient count and no treatment revenue is attached to this account.

No show rate and no consult outcome

The CRM’s appointment records may be quoted as appointment records — never as consults, never as a show rate, never as a filled calendar.

No blended cost per lead

Cost per lead is quoted per campaign and per service line. Dividing programme spend by programme leads would blend four different buyers into one meaningless number.

On-image social proof in the creative is the client’s claim

Customer counts, star ratings and endorsements that appear inside the ad artwork are the clinic’s statements, not his.
