Medical weight loss · United States

A weight-loss clinic opening, filling a calendar from zero

Everything had to come from cold traffic and survive a follow-up process the clinic had never run, staffed by people who had never run it.

The problem

What this business was actually up against.

A new clinic opened with no patient list, no reputation in the market, and a calendar that had to fill in weeks rather than quarters.

The product makes it harder. Medically supervised weight loss is a programme sold to someone who has usually already tried and failed at several other things — and cheap leads in the category are close to worthless.

So the build had to do two things at once: bring in people who would actually buy a programme, and move them onto a calendar fast enough that they were still interested when the phone rang.

What I built

The mechanism, step by step.

Not a list of tools. The decisions that produced the outcome, in the order they were made.

1

A priced entry offer as the qualifying mechanism

A weekly price point stated in the offer itself filters out free-consultation shoppers before they reach the calendar. The follow-up sequence is named for the offer it serves, so the channel, the offer and the price are recorded in text chosen at build time.

2

Two calendars matched to how the clinic actually operates

A pre-pay path and a first-visit path, kept as separate booking objects rather than collapsed into one — because they are two different commitments and behave differently.

3

A rebuilt automation architecture, deployed as a system

Generation two of a productised delivery system — lifecycle, follow-up and recovery engines — later rolled out to three more accounts. Not automations improvised per client.

4

Booking, confirmation and internal alerting as separate instrumented steps

Confirmation raises a task in the delivery team’s project tool and posts to Slack, so a booking is visible to a human rather than only to a database.

5

A cancellation recovery loop

Fall-outs are pulled back into sequence instead of leaking. Spam-removal hygiene ships as a standard build component.

6

Measurement on a versioned reporting template

A live instance of the reusable client reporting master sheet, not a one-off spreadsheet. The claim exists because the reporting existed first.

The boundary

What this case does not claim.

This section exists because it is the part that survives an interview. Stating the limits before anyone has to ask is what makes the rest of the page worth believing.

“Booked” means scheduled

Never attended, never held, never showed. No show rate is stated for this account because the tracker’s status column was not extracted — so the honest answer is that it is not known, not that it was good.

No revenue and no return on ad spend

A source document written during the engagement carries a return-on-spend headline. It is never repeated, because it is the client’s number and not his.

No cost per lead and no cost per appointment

The ads ran on a Meta account outside current access. No acquisition-cost figure is claimed for this engagement at all.

The grand-opening framing is founder-attested

It is Alex’s account of the engagement rather than a field in a system, and it is labelled that way rather than presented as data.
