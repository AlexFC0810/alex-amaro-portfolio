Chiropractic · United States

Head-to-head against an incumbent national agency, on the same ad account

Almost every marketing case study is uncontrolled — different budget, different market, different season, no counterfactual. This one has one, and it is unusually clean.

9.5× the leads, at ~75% lower cost per lead — same account 114 leads at $12.59 against the incumbent’s 12 at $49.81. Their best campaign, not their worst — the comparison most favourable to them.

The problem

What this business was actually up against.

A chiropractic practice was already paying an incumbent national agency — not a freelancer, an established multi-client shop with a category specialism — to run its Meta patient acquisition. The account was not producing.

Their best-performing campaign was returning leads at $49.81. Their two website-conversion campaigns were worse. For a local clinic, that is the difference between a paid-acquisition channel and an expense line: before any show rate, before any conversion to a paying patient, the arithmetic does not close.

Alex took the account over. Their campaigns and his now sit side by side in the same ad account, under the same client, in the same currency, in one lifetime pull — which removes almost every variable a skeptic would reach for.

What I built

The mechanism, step by step.

Not a list of tools. The decisions that produced the outcome, in the order they were made.

1

Changed the objective, and stopped paying for a conversion the account could not learn

The incumbent had split budget across a lead-generation campaign and two website-conversions campaigns — an optimisation target with too little conversion volume to ever train, burning budget on a learning phase it could not exit. Alex consolidated acquisition into a single Meta instant lead-form campaign. The change is visible in the campaign names in the account itself.

2

Rebuilt the offer around a named clinical modality, not “chiropractic care”

The winning campaign is built on a specific, differentiated, non-surgical treatment the practice actually owned — rather than the generic adjust-and-wellness pitch every competing clinic in the market was also running. Category-generic offers are what push a local cost per lead toward $49.81.

3

Wrote and produced the creative himself

Direct-response static built to the offer: pain-state hook, plain-language explanation of the modality, consultation call to action. The finished ad set is retained and independently corroborates the campaign name — the creative and the platform label were produced in two different systems and agree.

4

Built a second funnel layer instead of one flat campaign

Alongside the prospecting lead-form campaign he ran retargeting against warm traffic — the cheapest lead source in the account, cheaper than his own prospecting.

5

Qualified the form rather than maximising raw submissions

Qualifying questions ahead of volume, so what arrives at the front desk is sales-ready rather than merely numerous. Founder-attested method; the cost-per-lead figures do not depend on it.

The boundary

What this case does not claim.

This section exists because it is the part that survives an interview. Stating the limits before anyone has to ask is what makes the rest of the page worth believing.

No revenue, no return on ad spend, no patient value

He claims the leads and the cost per lead. He had no post-booking visibility into this practice’s schedule, close rate or case value, and closing is client-side. Any revenue figure attached to this case would be someone else’s number.

No booked-appointment count for this account

The vault holds hard booked-appointment trackers for other clients. It does not hold an opened, verified one for this practice. The claim stops at leads and cost per lead.

Not a simultaneous split test

“Head-to-head” means same account, same client, same service — not the same calendar weeks under a controlled split. This is a takeover comparison inside one lifetime pull. Anyone framing it as a live A/B is over-reading it.

No account-blended cost per lead

Cost per lead is quoted per campaign, always. The account totals exist to reconcile two independent pulls, not to be divided into a headline number.
