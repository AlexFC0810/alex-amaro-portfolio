03 · The gate

Most generations don’t make it.

The wall has a bouncer. Every AI generation is logged to a manifest with its model, its named test variable and a verdict — and the reject pile is the part most portfolios would hide.

109

generations logged in the manifest.

12

accepted on craft.

The hook in the reel above is one of them.

25

rejected — each with a written reason.

The notes are specific or they are useless: a phone visible in a shot that specified none, a sign that would not spell, a delivery measured slow against its own word budget. The note is the asset — the next brief inherits it.

72

still in the queue, unjudged.

Logged is not shipped. Volume only counts after the verdict.

The films run the same discipline: a beat-sheet manifest per cut, then measured gates — an integrated-loudness window, a longest-gap check on speech pacing, letterbox and edge-density sweeps. A cut that misses a gate goes back, not out. Behind the wall’s three controlled tests sit 516 prompts across six export files, 350 of them unique — one written brief and one named variable per test.

This is what “creative volume” means here: not more generations — more judged generations. The generation systems stay in-house; what they produce is what you just scrolled past.

Autonomous Opportunity Radar

TypeScript, on a 30-minute GitHub Actions cron. It has no public URL because it holds live inbox and Slack credentials, so instead of a screenshot, here is the pipeline:

saved-search email
   ↓ IMAP, skip anything tagged $RadarDone
parse  → title · URL · budget
   ↓
score  → apply / screen / skip   (the rubric is the IP)
   ↓
draft  → Claude, prompt-cached templates
   ↓
post   → Slack, proposal attached
   ↓
mark   → set $RadarDone   (dedup survives a stateless cron)

The judgment call is the interesting part. Version one read the official API — clean, but the key sits behind a manual approval gate that can stall for months, and once did. A perfect input that never turns on is worth zero. So the input became the inbox instead: no approval needed, unambiguously within terms, and that single change also deleted the dedup database, the bot and the always-on server. The workflow also ships dormant-safe — with no secrets set it exits green with a notice rather than failing every 30 minutes, so it arms itself the day the credentials land.

What this page does not claim

  • Not client revenue, and not ROAS. I do not have post-booking visibility, and closing is the client’s side of the line. What I claim is leads, booked appointments and the follow-up systems between them.
  • Not a blended cost per lead. Total spend divided by total leads is not a CPL and I will not quote one.
  • Not scaled AI voice. Shipped, and ran, from February 2025 — at single-digit to low-double enrolment volumes.
  • Not client campaigns, where the label says otherwise. The films and concept stills are own-brand studio work for products I own, and each caption says so.
  • Not client identities. Clients appear by vertical and country only.
  • The calculator claims nothing at all. It is your arithmetic on your inputs, with its assumptions labelled as assumptions.
